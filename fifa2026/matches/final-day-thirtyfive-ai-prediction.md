---

Fifty-six days after these two were supposed to meet in Qatar and didn't, they meet in New Jersey for everything. The **2026 Finalissima** — Euro champions v Copa América champions, booked for Lusail on March 27 — died in a venue dispute after Qatar suspended sport in the spring, UEFA proposing the Bernabéu, CONMEBOL the Monumental, nobody blinking. The World Cup has now rescheduled it with a better trophy: **Spain v Argentina, MetLife Stadium, Sunday** — the first World Cup final ever played between the two, the reigning European champions against the reigning world champions, and **Lionel Messi's last World Cup match**, at 39, two goals clear of nobody: he and Mbappé sit tied on eight, Messi ahead only on assists.

The machine has skin in this game. Its bracket sent Spain here weeks ago — it has called every Spanish knockout win, including the semi-final over France — but its Day 33 verdict, England past Argentina on penalties, was the tournament's one miss, and so Sunday's opponent is the team the machine eliminated and reality didn't. We handed the fixture to **Agent Pitch**, our AI soccer simulator in which all 22 players are autonomous agents — each running its own strategy code, reading the game state tick by tick, deciding for itself whether to press, pass, hold or shoot. The machine's verdict: **Spain 1–0**, the winner scored by the youngest player on the pitch, in the fixture the oldest one came to win.

### The Teams
**Spain** arrive as the tournament's machine within the machine: seven games unbeaten, **thirteen scored, one conceded, six clean sheets** — the last of them a 2–0 semi-final dismantling of France in Arlington, Oyarzabal converting from the spot after Digne fouled Yamal and Pedro Porro finishing a give-and-go off Olmo on 58 minutes, a night Mbappé never managed a shot on target. **Luis de la Fuente** has no reason to touch a thing and reportedly won't: the same XI starts a third consecutive knockout game — **Unai Simón; Cucurella, Laporte, Cubarsí, Porro; Fabián Ruiz, Rodri; Baena, Olmo, Yamal; Oyarzabal** — with no suspensions, Yamal's late-semi-final knock cleared, and Yéremy Pino the only absentee. Behind them waits the tournament's most decisive bench: Mikel Merino has won Spain two knockout rounds after coming on.

**Argentina** are here because their bench rescued the semi-final their starting shape lost. **Lionel Scaloni's** 4-3-3 experiment in Atlanta misfired — Anthony Gordon put England ahead on 55 — before the recalibration: **Enzo Fernández** bent in an equaliser from outside the box on 85 and **Lautaro Martínez** turned in Messi's pass in stoppage time, both goals assisted by the captain. For the final, every credible projection restores the proven 4-4-2: **Emiliano Martínez; Tagliafico, Lisandro Martínez, Romero, Molina; Mac Allister, Enzo Fernández, Paredes, De Paul; Álvarez, Messi** — De Paul back for Simeone, Álvarez keeping the shirt with Lautaro held as the closer he has twice proven to be. No bans, no injuries, and one enormous number: Argentina are ninety minutes from becoming the first nation since Brazil in 1962 to retain the World Cup.

### Historical Backdrop
Fourteen meetings since 1952 and the ledger is dead level — six wins apiece, two draws — but the two facts that matter most are absences: they have met just **once** at a World Cup, a 2–1 Argentina win in the 1966 group stage at Wembley, and **never** in a final of anything — the one final they were given, the 2026 Finalissima, was cancelled before it could be played. The most recent meeting is a Spanish scar on Argentine memory: **6–1 in Madrid in March 2018**, Isco's hat-trick against a Messi-less Argentina. Spain have won three of the four meetings this century; Argentina's last win over La Roja with Messi on the pitch was the 4–3 friendly in 2010. Sunday settles half a century of near-misses with the biggest prize the sport owns.

### Simulation Report — **Spain 1–0 Argentina**
The machine's final was a duel between one team's system and one goalkeeper's evening. Spain controlled it without ever running away with it — **53% possession, near-identical in both halves (52–48, then 54–46)** — and aimed everything through the man Argentina's gameplan was built to stop: **Lamine Yamal took twelve of Spain's fourteen shots**, eight of them before half-time, beginning in the 10th minute and arriving from the same right half-space Argentina kept conceding and Emiliano Martínez kept closing. Baena from the left on 39 and Oyarzabal on 51 were the only other Spaniards to try; **Dibu Martínez repelled the first eleven attempts he faced**. Argentina's answer was patient and increasingly real: a single first-half shot became six after the break, **Julián Álvarez** testing Simón on 53, 60 and 68 before **Messi's one shot of the final arrived on 73** — saved. Three minutes later the match ended as the machine's whole tournament has trended: Lisandro Martínez got a foot in and couldn't end the move, the ball came back to Yamal in the pocket, and **his twelfth attempt of the night finally beat Dibu — 1–0, 76 minutes**. Argentina threw Álvarez at it twice more (83', 88'); Spain's line — six clean sheets coming in — held for a seventh. Full time: **14 shots to 7, 56 tackle contests to 59**, and no need for the extra half-hour or the shootout the engine stood ready to play.

**AI takeaway:** The machine crowned the team it has believed in since the group stage, and did it in the team's own image — a final won 1–0 with the ball, the line held, and one truly special player trusted to keep shooting until the eleventh save couldn't become a twelfth. The through-line of the machine's knockout rounds — *volume doesn't score, nerve does* — meets its exception at last: Yamal's volume WAS the nerve, twelve attempts without a flinch at nineteen years old. For Argentina the simulation is precise and cruel: the champions were never overrun — they out-tackled Spain and grew all second half — but Messi's farewell produced one shot, one save, and a Golden Boot decided elsewhere: stuck on eight, he watches Mbappé — whom the machine's own bronze-final sim had scoring his ninth on Saturday — take the prize on the machine's combined ledger. **Spain, per the machine: world champions, holders of the Euro and World Cup at once — the double only they themselves have ever held.**
