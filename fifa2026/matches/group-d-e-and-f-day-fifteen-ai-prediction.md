# Gyökeres and Gakpo Strike Twice, and Türkiye Sink a Rotated USA — AI's Day Fifteen

*An Agent Pitch simulation special — every player an AI agent, every decision its own code. We ran the World Cup's fifteenth day through the machine. Here is what it saw.*

**Filed: June 24, 2026 — on the eve of the final group games**

---

Day fifteen carries the final round onward — matchday three, where the two fixtures in a group kick off **simultaneously** so no one can play to a known result. Three more pools settle their fate on **Thursday, June 25**: **Group D**, **Group E** and **Group F** — six matches across two coasts. **Group E** goes first at **4:00 PM ET**: **Curaçao vs Ivory Coast** at Lincoln Financial Field in Philadelphia and **Ecuador vs Germany** at MetLife Stadium. **Group F** follows at **7:00 PM ET**: **Japan vs Sweden** at AT&T Stadium in Arlington and **Tunisia vs Netherlands** at Arrowhead Stadium in Kansas City. Then **Group D** closes the night at **10:00 PM ET** on the West Coast: **Türkiye vs United States** at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood and **Paraguay vs Australia** at Levi's Stadium in Santa Clara.

As ever, we handed all six fixtures to **Agent Pitch**, our AI soccer simulator in which all 22 players are autonomous agents — each running its own strategy code, reading the game state tick by tick, and deciding for itself whether to press, pass, hold or shoot. No scripts, no scripted endings. And the machine served up its loudest day of the final round — clinical favourites in Group E, chaos in Group F, and a brace of must-win upsets in Group D. In Philadelphia, **Ivory Coast beat Curaçao 1–0**. At MetLife, **Germany's rotated side won 3–1 at Ecuador**, Jamie Leweling scoring twice. In Arlington, the game of the round: **Japan and Sweden drew 2–2**, Viktor Gyökeres striking twice but Japan hitting back. In Kansas City, **the Netherlands won 2–1 at Tunisia** behind a Cody Gakpo brace. At SoFi, **Türkiye beat a much-changed United States 1–0**. And in Santa Clara, **Paraguay won the knockout 1–0**, dumping Australia out.

## Match 1 — Curaçao vs Ivory Coast

### Match Info
- **Competition:** 2026 FIFA World Cup, Group E (Matchday 3)

### The Teams
**Curaçao**, under the veteran **Dick Advocaat**, are the smallest nation by population ever to reach a World Cup, and they must win to keep alive their fairytale. They set up to frustrate in a **4-3-3** that folds into a deep 4-1-4-1 block: **Eloy Room** in goal; a back four of **Sherel Floranus**, **Armando Obispo**, **Riechedly Bazoer** and **Deveron Fonville**; the **Bacuna** brothers (Leandro and Juninho) with **Livano Comenencia** in midfield; and **Tahith Chong**, **Jürgen Locadia** and **Sontje Hansen** carrying the counter. **Ivory Coast**, under **Emerse Faé**, need only a draw to go through as runners-up and arrive in a **4-3-3**. **Yahia Fofana** in goal; **Ghislain Konan**, **Emmanuel Agbadou**, **Odilon Kossounou** and **Guéla Doué** (in for the injured Singo) across the back; captain **Franck Kessié** anchoring with **Ibrahim Sangaré** and **Christ Inao Oulaï**; and a pacy front three of **Yan Diomande**, **Ange-Yoan Bonny** and **Amad Diallo**. Use the ball, work the wings, and take the chances their quality should bring.

### Historical Backdrop
The two nations have **never met** in a senior men's international, so this Group E finale is a genuine first-ever encounter — and a debut World Cup meeting between Curaçao and an African side. Two-time Africa Cup of Nations winners Ivory Coast bring far the deeper pedigree; for Curaçao, simply being here, a Caribbean island of around 150,000 people, is the story.

### Simulation Report — **Curaçao 0–1 Ivory Coast**
A siege that took until the final quarter to break. Ivory Coast did everything but score for an hour — **60% of the ball, eighteen shots to five** — as Curaçao's deep block and a heroic Eloy Room held the line, the islanders throwing themselves into a remarkable **108 tackle contests**. **Yan Diomande** was relentless and wasteful in equal measure, firing **thirteen** of the Ivorians' eighteen attempts without reward; it was the calmer head that settled it. On **68'**, **Ange-Yoan Bonny** found the breakthrough for **1–0**, and Faé's side saw out the nervy finish to claim the point — and the goal — they needed. **AI takeaway:** The machine reads a classic favourite-grinds-down-minnow, with a twist of profligacy: Diomande's thirteen shots are the kind of one-man barrage that usually ends in frustration, and nearly did. Bonny's single clinical moment is the difference between Ivorian qualification and a Curaçao smash-and-grab. For Advocaat's islanders, eighteen shots faced and only one conceded is a badge of honour — but it ends a World Cup that already rewrote the record books just by beginning.
