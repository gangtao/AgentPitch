# Doku's Double, Yamal Breaks the Siege, and Iran Win Without the Ball — AI Calls Day Five

*An Agent Pitch simulation special — every player an AI agent, every decision its own code. We ran the World Cup's fifth day through the machine. Here is what it saw.*

**Filed: June 13, 2026 — two days before kickoff**

---

Day five unwraps the tournament's last two groups. **Group H** — Spain's group of death-by-possession — opens in the southern heat: **Spain vs Cape Verde** at Mercedes-Benz Stadium in Atlanta, then the heavyweight rematch **Saudi Arabia vs Uruguay** under the Miami sun. **Group G**, the De Bruyne-and-Salah group, runs up the West Coast: **Belgium vs Egypt** at Seattle's Lumen Field, and the day's closer, **Iran vs New Zealand**, beneath the SoFi Stadium roof in Los Angeles.

As ever, we handed all four fixtures to **Agent Pitch**, our AI soccer simulator in which all 22 players are autonomous agents — each running its own strategy code, reading the game state tick by tick, and deciding for itself whether to press, pass, hold or shoot. No scripts, no scripted endings. The machine's verdict on day five: Spain lay siege but win by a single Yamal goal, Belgium turn a tight line into a four-goal afternoon through a rampant Doku, Uruguay's press strangles Saudi Arabia, and in Los Angeles Iran win the match while New Zealand win the ball — the tournament's "possession buys nothing" curse claiming one more believer.

## Match 1 — Spain vs Cape Verde

### Match Info
- **Competition:** 2026 FIFA World Cup, Group H (Matchday 1)
- **Date / Time:** Monday, June 15, 2026 — 9:00 AM local
- **Venue:** Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Atlanta, Georgia
- **Broadcast:** FOX, Peacock
- **Market line:** Spain −1200, O/U 3.5

### The Teams
**Spain** arrive as reigning European champions and FIFA's No. 2 side, and **Luis de la Fuente** keeps the 4-3-3 that won Euro 2024: **Rodri** the metronome at the base, **Pedri** and **Fabián Ruiz** as the interiors, and the two most direct wingers in world football either side of **Mikel Oyarzabal** — **Nico Williams** running the left touchline, captain-in-waiting **Lamine Yamal** cutting inside off the right onto his left foot. Possession is not a tactic here; it is the whole religion.

**Cape Verde** are **World Cup debutants** — an Atlantic archipelago of barely half a million people — and **Bubista** has built them to survive: a 4-2-3-1 that folds into a narrow low block, **Logan Costa** of Villarreal the standout ball-playing centre-back, **Jamiro Monteiro** the creative outlet, all-time top scorer and captain **Ryan Mendes** drifting in off the right, and **Dailon Livramento** alone up top to chase the long clearances. The plan: keep eleven behind the ball and counter on a prayer.

### Historical Backdrop
There is no history at all — this is the **first meeting between Spain and Cape Verde at any level**, a fixture made possible only by Cape Verde's maiden qualification. On one side, the 2010 world champions and Euro 2024 holders; on the other, one of the smallest nations ever to reach a World Cup, with no head-to-head page to consult because none has ever been needed.

### Simulation Report — **Spain 1–0 Cape Verde**

The machine produced the siege everyone expected and the scoreline almost nobody did. Spain held **70% of the ball** and out-shot Cape Verde **28 to 1** — and still needed a single moment of Yamal to separate them.

It came on **21 minutes**. With Cape Verde's two banks already set and refusing to widen, **Lamine Yamal** did what the whole system is built to let him do: drift into the right half-space, take the ball on the half-turn, and beat **Vozinha** — **1–0**, one of his six shots, and as it turned out the only one of Spain's twenty-eight that mattered. Behind him, **Mikel Oyarzabal** had the afternoon of a striker on a different wavelength to the scoreboard: **fifteen shots, no goals**, every one of them swallowed by the debutants' block or by Vozinha. **Nico Williams** added six more from the left. Cape Verde, for their part, managed **a single shot all match** — a lone Ryan Mendes effort on the break — and spent the other 89 minutes defending for their World Cup lives.

What the 1–0 hides is how well the plan worked for the side that lost. Bubista asked for eleven behind the ball and a clean sheet to chase; he got 89 minutes of it and conceded once. The market wanted four goals and a procession (O/U 3.5); the machine delivered a nervy single-goal grind that stayed well under the line. Final whistle: **Spain 1, Cape Verde 0**.

**AI takeaway:** The model sees the favourite winning and the debutant passing its first audition. Oyarzabal shooting fifteen times without scoring is the recurring tax on a low block that never breaks shape — Spain got the three points but only through the one player good enough to invent a goal from nothing. Cape Verde lost the match and won the argument: that block will trouble Uruguay and Saudi Arabia too.

## Match 2 — Belgium vs Egypt

### Match Info
- **Competition:** 2026 FIFA World Cup, Group G (Matchday 1)
- **Date / Time:** Monday, June 15, 2026 — 12:00 PM local
- **Venue:** Lumen Field, Seattle, Washington
- **Broadcast:** FOX, Peacock
- **Market line:** Belgium −155, O/U 2.5

### The Teams
**Belgium** under **Rudi García** are a golden generation in transition, still lethal in the front third: **Kevin De Bruyne** — now the captain — pulling strings from the right half-space, **Jérémy Doku** isolating fullbacks one-on-one down the left, **Romelu Lukaku** the central focal point, **Leandro Trossard** cutting in from the right, and **Thibaut Courtois** behind a back four screened by the **Tielemans–Onana** double pivot. The caveat, as ever: this ageing core wins games in bursts, not by pressing for ninety minutes.

**Egypt** make their first World Cup since 2018 with **Hossam Hassan's** compact 4-3-3, built to stay tight and release fast into the two men who decide everything: captain **Mohamed Salah** off the right and **Omar Marmoush**, now of Manchester City, through the middle. **Mahmoud Trezeguet** gives the left a direct outlet, **Emam Ashour** is the advanced creator, and the whole side is wired to spend long stretches deep before springing the counter.

### Historical Backdrop
The sides have met three times, all friendlies — never in a competitive match. Egypt thrashed Belgium **4–0** in Cairo in 2005, Belgium replied with a **3–0** win in a 2018 World Cup warm-up, and Egypt took the most recent meeting **2–1** in 2022, leaving the friendly ledger 2–1 in the Pharaohs' favour. Seattle is the first time the two have ever played a match that counts.

### Simulation Report — **Belgium 4–1 Egypt**

The day's one goalfest, and the day's one match to clear its over/under without breaking stride. On a near-even possession split — **Belgium 54%, Egypt 46%** — the favourites simply had more teeth in the final third, out-shooting Egypt **18 to 7** and turning four of those chances into goals.

The torment came down the left, and its name was **Jérémy Doku**. He fired **twelve** of Belgium's eighteen shots and scored two of them: the opener on **6 minutes**, slashing in off the flank past **Mohamed El Shenawy**, and again on **37 minutes** to make it 3–1. In between, on **35 minutes**, **Leandro Trossard** cut in from the right to finish for 2–1, and on **56 minutes** **Romelu Lukaku** added the fourth from close range — the classic García pattern of De Bruyne and Doku manufacturing, Lukaku converting.

Egypt were not passive, and they were not embarrassed. **Salah** got three shots away off the right; **Marmoush** matched Lukaku with four efforts of his own and took the one that counted — a **89th-minute** consolation, the last kick of note, to deny Courtois a clean sheet and make it **4–1**. But the Pharaohs' best men were chasing the game from the sixth minute, and the model never had them level after that. Final whistle: **Belgium 4, Egypt 1**.

**AI takeaway:** The machine reads this as the favourite's attack clicking in exactly the bursts García relies on — Doku's twelve-shot afternoon is the difference between Belgium and almost anyone else in Group G. Egypt will be encouraged that Salah and Marmoush both found shooting room and that they out-tackled nobody yet still created seven chances; they will be alarmed that conceding early turned a tight game into a four-goal one. In a group this open, an opening-day battering is a heavy way to start.

## Match 3 — Saudi Arabia vs Uruguay

### Match Info
- **Competition:** 2026 FIFA World Cup, Group H (Matchday 1)
- **Date / Time:** Monday, June 15, 2026 — 3:00 PM local
- **Venue:** Hard Rock Stadium, Miami Gardens, Florida
- **Broadcast:** FS1, Peacock
- **Market line:** Uruguay −230, O/U 2.5

### The Teams
**Saudi Arabia**, now coached by **Georgios Donis** after Hervé Renard's exit, set up in a disciplined 4-2-3-1 designed to frustrate and counter: a **Kanno–Al-Khaibari** double pivot shielding the back four, talisman **Salem Al-Dawsari** carrying the threat from the left, and **Firas Al-Buraikan** alone up front. With first-choice keeper Nawaf Al-Aqidi injured, **Mohammed Al-Owais** returns in goal. The blueprint is the one that famously beat Argentina in 2022: stay compact, spring on the turnover.

**Uruguay** are **Marcelo Bielsa's** most intense side yet — a 4-3-3 built on vertical, man-to-man pressing and relentless forward running. Captain **Federico Valverde** drives from midfield, **Manuel Ugarte** destroys in front of the back four, **Giorgian de Arrascaeta** drifts in from the left to create, and **Darwin Núñez** leads the line as both the first presser and the final shot. Behind them, **Ronald Araújo** and **José María Giménez** form one of the tournament's most physical centre-back pairs.

### Historical Backdrop
A genuine rematch with pedigree. The sides have met three times, most memorably in the **2018 World Cup group stage in Russia**, where Uruguay won **1–0** through Luis Suárez to send La Celeste through and effectively eliminate the Saudis. Earlier friendlies split honours — Saudi Arabia shocked Uruguay **3–2** in 2002, and a 2014 meeting finished **1–1** — but the only competitive result between them belongs to Uruguay.

### Simulation Report — **Saudi Arabia 0–2 Uruguay**

Bielsa's machine did to Saudi Arabia what Bielsa's machines do: it took the ball, took the territory, and squeezed until something gave. Uruguay held **60% possession**, out-shot the Saudis **21 to 1**, and out-tackled them **100 to 86** — the most combative defensive performance the simulator has logged this tournament, a press that simply never switched off.

For an hour the Donis block held, and **Mohammed Al-Owais** kept answering. Then the pressure told. On **52 minutes**, **Darwin Núñez** — who finished with a barely-believable **thirteen shots** — converted one of them for **1–0**, the reward for living on the last defender's shoulder all afternoon. On **65 minutes**, **Giorgian de Arrascaeta** drifted in from the left and doubled it, **2–0**, the creator turning finisher. Núñez kept firing to the end; **Valverde** and **Pellistri** chipped in further efforts; the only surprise was that the margin stayed at two.

Saudi Arabia's afternoon is told by a single number: **one shot**, a lone **Firas Al-Buraikan** effort, against twenty-one at the other end. The compact block bought them an hour of resistance and the counter never came — Uruguay's man-marking smothered Al-Dawsari before he could turn. Final whistle: **Saudi Arabia 0, Uruguay 2**.

**AI takeaway:** The model delivers the form line cleanly — the −230 favourite controls, presses and wins, and the underdog's lone-shot evening is the price of an hour spent purely surviving. Núñez's thirteen-shot, one-goal return is wasteful enough to worry Bielsa and ruthless enough to win the game; de Arrascaeta's quality settled it. Saudi Arabia kept the score respectable, but a group with both Spain and Uruguay in it just got very steep.

## Match 4 — Iran vs New Zealand

### Match Info
- **Competition:** 2026 FIFA World Cup, Group G (Matchday 1)
- **Date / Time:** Monday, June 15, 2026 — 6:00 PM local
- **Venue:** SoFi Stadium, Inglewood, California
- **Broadcast:** FS1, Peacock
- **Market line:** Iran −110, O/U 2.5

### The Teams
**Iran**, under **Amir Ghalenoei**, are the tournament's archetypal organised counter-punchers: a 4-2-3-1 that collapses into a compact low block and breaks fast into captain and talisman **Mehdi Taremi**. **Saeid Ezatolahi** and **Saman Ghoddos** anchor midfield, **Mehdi Ghayedi** is the dribble-first number ten, and **Alireza Jahanbakhsh** provides the cut-inside threat from the right. The one nag heading in: Sardar Azmoun's omission leaves no natural deputy for Taremi.

**New Zealand**, managed by **Darren Bazeley**, are the lowest-ranked side in the field and play to their strengths: a 4-3-3 that drops into a deep 4-5-1 block and goes direct to the talisman, captain **Chris Wood**. **Sarpreet Singh** is the creative spark, **Marko Stamenic** the set-piece deliverer, and **Liberato Cacace** the one fullback licensed to fly forward. The All Whites' currency is physicality, second balls, and anything that drops near Wood.

### Historical Backdrop
This one is a blank page: **Iran and New Zealand have essentially never met.** No database returns a recorded senior international between them — unsurprising for two nations from different confederations (Iran from Asia's AFC, New Zealand from Oceania's OFC) whose qualifying paths never crossed. The SoFi Stadium meeting will, in all likelihood, be the first match these countries have ever played.

### Simulation Report — **Iran 2–0 New Zealand**

The day's quiet lesson in the difference between having the ball and using it. **New Zealand finished with more possession — 53% to Iran's 47%** — and lost by two, undone by the side that needed fewer touches and took its chances.

The match turned on the boots of **Mehdi Ghayedi**, and on his efficiency above all. Iran's busiest shooter was actually **Alireza Jahanbakhsh**, who carved out **seven** efforts from the right without reward; Ghayedi, by contrast, took just **three** all match and scored with two of them. The first arrived on **25 minutes**, the dribbling number ten finishing a transition past **Max Crocombe**; the second on **71 minutes** sealed it, **2–0**, the same player again the coolest head in a frantic box. Taremi added three shots as the focal point but it was the man behind him who punished the All Whites.

New Zealand did exactly what Bazeley's blueprint demands — they saw plenty of the ball and went route-one toward **Chris Wood**, who got two shots away, with **Matthew Garbett** adding three more from the right. But Iran's block did its job: 53% of the ball turned into seven shots and no goals, and the All Whites never found the knockdown that springs Wood clean. Iran out-tackled them **68 to 37** and were sharper in both boxes. Final whistle: **Iran 2, New Zealand 0**.

**AI takeaway:** The model's read is the one this tournament keeps writing: possession without penetration is worth nothing, and a compact side that breaks clinically beats a patient one that does not. Ghayedi scoring twice from three shots while Jahanbakhsh blazed seven is the whole match in a sentence — Iran were the more ruthless team, not the busier one. New Zealand will rue a 53%-of-the-ball afternoon that produced no goals; in a group with Belgium and Egypt, chances this scarce will not be forgiven.
