# Lautaro Settles It Early, Mbappé Blanks on a Dozen Shots — AI's Day Twelve

*An Agent Pitch simulation special — every player an AI agent, every decision its own code. We ran the World Cup's twelfth day through the machine. Here is what it saw.*

**Filed: June 20, 2026 — two days before kickoff**

---

Day twelve brings the second round of group fixtures back into **Group I** and **Group J**, the tournament's final pair of pools, last visited on day six. Matchday two is where the openers get answered — and in the real world the first round went entirely to seed: Argentina, Austria, France and Norway all won, leaving Senegal, Iraq, Jordan and Algeria already chasing. Monday, June 22 runs from a Texas lunchtime to a late kickoff under the Levi's Stadium lights in Santa Clara. **Group J** bookends the day: holders **Argentina vs Austria** at AT&T Stadium in Arlington to open, then **Jordan vs Algeria** in California to close. **Group I** fills the middle: **France vs Iraq** at Lincoln Financial Field in Philadelphia, and **Norway vs Senegal** at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey.

As ever, we handed all four fixtures to **Agent Pitch**, our AI soccer simulator in which all 22 players are autonomous agents — each running its own strategy code, reading the game state tick by tick, and deciding for itself whether to press, pass, hold or shoot. No scripts, no scripted endings. And the machine's day twelve was kinder to the favourites than the openers had threatened: in Arlington, **Argentina beat Austria 1–0**, Lautaro Martínez striking early and the holders defending it out against a relentless Austrian press. In Philadelphia, **France beat Iraq 2–0** — but the story was **Kylian Mbappé blanking on twelve shots**, with Bradley Barcola and Ousmane Dembélé doing the finishing instead. At MetLife, **Senegal won 2–0 at Norway**, Ismaïla Sarr and Sadio Mané punishing a profligate Erling Haaland. And in Santa Clara, **Algeria edged Jordan 1–0**, Mohamed Amoura's early strike enough for a controlling night.

## Match 1 — Argentina vs Austria

### The Teams
**Argentina**, under **Lionel Scaloni**, arrive as world champions and full of running after a **Lionel Messi hat-trick sank Algeria 3–0** in their opener. The 4-3-3 is built around the **Rodrigo De Paul–Enzo Fernández–Alexis Mac Allister** midfield, with **Messi** drifting in off the right, **Lautaro Martínez** through the middle and **Thiago Almada** off the left. The back line pairs **Cristian Romero** and **Lisandro Martínez**, with **Facundo Medina** and **Gonzalo Montiel** as the full-backs. Control the game, feed Messi, and make it two from two.

**Austria**, under **Ralf Rangnick**, arrive in form and fearless after stunning Jordan **3–1** for their first World Cup win in 36 years. The 4-2-3-1 is a gegenpressing machine: **Nicolas Seiwald** and **Xaver Schlager** screen, **David Alaba** captains from centre-back, **Marcel Sabitzer** pulls the strings at the tip, and the wide pair of **Romano Schmid** and **Patrick Wimmer** run in behind for the target man **Marko Arnautović**. Press high, win it back early, and hit the holders before they settle.

### Historical Backdrop
Argentina and Austria have crossed paths only rarely, and **never at a World Cup** — this is their first meeting on the sport's biggest stage. Their best-documented previous encounters were both friendlies on Austrian soil: a **5–1 Argentina rout in Vienna in 1980**, famous for a Diego Maradona hat-trick, and a 1–1 draw in 1990 (some 2026 previews list no prior meetings at all, a measure of how thin and old the record is). Both arrive off opening wins — holders Argentina dismantled Algeria 3–0 behind a **Lionel Messi hat-trick**, while Austria stunned debutants Jordan 3–1 for their **first World Cup victory in 36 years**. On paper a mismatch; in confidence, perhaps less so.

**AI takeaway:** A controlled, professional win for the champions — nothing like the swagger of their opening hat-trick night, but a clean sheet and three points all the same. Austria will rue a familiar tale: plenty of the ball, plenty of running, and no end product against a well-organised box. The holders are up and running; Rangnick's men leave Arlington with nothing to show for a busy afternoon.

The form lines diverge too: France opened with a commanding **3–1 win over Senegal**, Kylian Mbappé scoring twice and Bradley Barcola adding the third, while Iraq were beaten **4–1 by Norway**, undone by an Erling Haaland brace despite Aymen Hussein getting on the scoresheet. The story here is the novelty of the meeting itself.
