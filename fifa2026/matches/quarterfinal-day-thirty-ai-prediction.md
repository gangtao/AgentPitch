Forty years after Belgium knocked Spain out of the 1986 quarter-final on penalties, Spain made sure this one never reached the spot.

### The Teams
**Spain**, under **Luis de la Fuente**, are the tournament's standard: five wins from five, **not a single goal conceded**, and the same **4-3-3** that beat Portugal in the last 16 — **Unai Simón** in goal; **Marc Cucurella**, **Aymeric Laporte**, **Pau Cubarsí** and **Pedro Porro** across the back; **Rodri** the single pivot with **Dani Olmo** and **Pedri** as the interiors; **Álex Baena**, **Mikel Oyarzabal** and **Lamine Yamal** in front. Nico Williams is back in the squad but starts on the bench. The plan does not change: 65-percent possession, full-backs ultra-high, and the Rodri–Pedri machine squeezing the life out of the game.

**Belgium**, under **Rudi García**, arrive on a genuine roll — a perfect nine-point group, a 120th-minute extra-time escape against Senegal, and then the 4–1 statement over the USA delivered with **Kevin De Bruyne and Jérémy Doku both on the bench**. De Bruyne is recalled for the quarter-final into a **4-2-3-1**: **Thibaut Courtois** in goal; **Maxim De Cuyper**, **Nathan Ngoy**, **Brandon Mechele** and **Timothy Castagne** in defence; a **Youri Tielemans**–**Hans Vanaken** double pivot reshaped by **Amadou Onana's tournament-ending ACL tear**; **Leandro Trossard**, De Bruyne and **Dodi Lukebakio** behind the form forward of the round of 16, **Charles De Ketelaere**. Doku stays a bench weapon. The bet is total: concede the ball, hold the mid-block, and make one or two transitions count.

### Historical Backdrop
Spain and Belgium have met **23 times: 12 Spanish wins, 5 Belgian, 6 draws**, and Spain are unbeaten in the last seven (six wins, one draw). But the two World Cup meetings are split — and the one that matters here is **the 1986 quarter-final in Mexico**, where Belgium held Spain 1–1 through extra time and went through **5–4 on penalties**, one of the great heists of the Golden-Generation prequel era. Spain took revenge 2–1 in the 1990 group stage, and the sides have not met at a World Cup since. Forty years on, the fixture returns at the same stage.

### Simulation Report — **Spain 2–1 Belgium (a.e.t.)**
The shock arrived early. On **15'**, with Spain's full-backs already pushed high, Belgium sprang exactly the transition García has built this team around — and **Leandro Trossard**, the inverted left winger, cut inside and finished. **It was the first goal Spain had conceded in the entire tournament**, and for the next fifty minutes the machine watched the favourites run into a wall: a compact 4-5-1 block, **Courtois** behind it, and a Belgian side perfectly content to live without the ball. Except they didn't just live without it — **Belgium held 47% possession**, far more of the game than anyone predicted against the Rodri machine, with De Bruyne dropping into pockets and Trossard (a team-high **six shots**) carrying the counter again and again.

Spain's response was relentless rather than fluent. The pattern of the evening — **22 Spanish shots to Belgium's 11**, and a ferocious counter-press that racked up **92 defensive contests to 73** — finally told on **65'**, and it came from the one player who had barely shot at all: **Dani Olmo**, the late-arriving interior, took his **only chance of the match** and buried it. 1–1, and the tie settled into exactly the shape Belgium wanted: ninety minutes done, extra time, the 1986 shootout lurking one half-hour away.

Belgium never got there. Through extra time Spain kept feeding their teenager — **Lamine Yamal finished with a match-high eleven shots**, Courtois and the block repelling ten of them — and on **109'** the eleventh landed: Yamal cut in off the right one final time and beat Courtois to make it **2–1**. Belgium, with barely twenty minutes of legs left and Doku's cameo spent chasing the game, could not find a second transition. This time there would be no penalties.

**AI takeaway:** The machine reads this as the strongest stress test the favourites have faced — and the clearest sign of why they remain favourites. Belgium executed García's plan almost perfectly: they scored first, dragged Spain's shutout streak to a halt, kept nearly half the ball, and carried the tie to the 109th minute. But "almost" is the whole margin in a quarter-final. Spain doubled Belgium's shot count, out-pressed them by fifty contests, and had the two most dangerous attackers on the pitch — Olmo, clinical off one chance, and Yamal, relentless off eleven. The 1986 echo died in extra time: this Spain does not wait for the coin-flip of a shootout. Belgium's tournament ends with heads high; Spain move into the semi-finals having finally been punched — and having answered.
