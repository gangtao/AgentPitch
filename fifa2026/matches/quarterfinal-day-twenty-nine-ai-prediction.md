### Historical Backdrop
The nations have met **six times, and France have won five** — Morocco's only victory came in a **1998 friendly**. The defining meeting is fresh and painful: the **2022 World Cup semi-final in Qatar**, which **France won 2–0** through an early **Théo Hernández** strike and a late **Randal Kolo Muani** tap-in, ending the greatest run in African World Cup history — Morocco had become the first African side ever to reach the last four. This is the rematch, on a new continent, with the Atlas Lions once again cast as the underdog with a point to prove.

### Simulation Report — **France 2–1 Morocco**
The favourites landed the first blow and then had to survive for it. On **19'**, **Kylian Mbappé** did what Mbappé does in these games — a single decisive moment, finished past Bounou to settle French nerves and reward a patient opening. But this was no procession. **Morocco carried the play for long stretches — 53% of the ball, and a relentless 51 defensive contests to France's 36** — pressing France's makeshift midfield and pushing **Achraf Hakimi** high down the right. **Ismael Saibari**, hamstring doubt and all, led the line with a match-high **seven shots**, but Bounou's opposite number was equal to the Moroccan striker every time. For an hour France held their 1–0 and squeezed, Koné and Rabiot screening in front of the back four.

Then, on **75'**, the block finally cracked: **Brahim Díaz** — Morocco's creator and first penalty taker — struck to level it at **1–1** and, for seven minutes, the machine had a shootout in its sights. It lasted exactly that long. On **82'**, **Ousmane Dembélé**, red-hot all tournament, answered for France to make it **2–1** — the winner arriving almost before Morocco had finished celebrating the equaliser. France's front four had done the damage: **Mbappé (7 shots), Dembélé (5) and Olise (3)** kept the pressure relentless off **16 shots to Morocco's 11**, and this time there was no way back for the Atlas Lions. France saw out the closing minutes to reach the semi-finals without needing the extra thirty.

**AI takeaway:** The machine reads this as a near-perfect distillation of why France keep winning these ties and Morocco keep just falling short. Morocco were the better-organised side for much of it — more ball, more running, Saibari a constant menace — and drew genuinely level with fifteen minutes to play. But France did not need to be the better team; they needed two moments, and Mbappé and Dembélé supplied them. The seven-minute swing from Brahim's equaliser to Dembélé's winner is the whole story: the underdog touched the semi-final and had it taken away almost instantly. As in 2022, the scoreline reads 2–1 rather than 2–0, but the ending is the same — France march on, Morocco go home wondering what a single held lead might have brought.
