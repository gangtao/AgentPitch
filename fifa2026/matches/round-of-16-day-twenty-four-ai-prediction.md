### Simulation Report — **Canada 1–3 Morocco**
The co-hosts landed the first blow and were then picked apart by a single ruthless finisher. Canada started brightly, and on **21'** captain **Jonathan David** gave NRG Stadium its dream start, sweeping in the opener. It lasted three minutes. **Ismael Saibari** — who would end the night with a barely believable **fourteen shots** — levelled on **24'**, struck again on **30'** to turn the game before half-time, and completed his hat-trick on **62'** to put it beyond reach. Morocco finished with **57% of the ball and an 18–12 shot edge**, Hakimi and Brahim Díaz threading the supply to a striker in the mood. Canada were not disgraced — **Oluwaseyi** (six shots) and David (four) kept Bounou honest, and a fit-again **Davies** raided the left all afternoon — but every time they pushed, Saibari answered at the other end.

**AI takeaway:** The machine reads this as the favourite's class told through one player: a centre-forward given a fraction of space between the lines and punishing it repeatedly, three of fourteen attempts finding the net. Canada's high-pressing 4-4-2 created real chances and struck first, but committing bodies forward against Morocco's counter left Saibari the room he needed, and the game turned on his ten-minute burst either side of the half-hour. For Marsch's co-hosts, a proud tournament ends on the sword of a striker's afternoon; for Morocco, ominously efficient, the sense that the deep run of 2022 was no fluke.

France (2022 finalists) arrived off a 3–0 dismissal of Sweden as the shortest-priced favourites of the entire knockout stage; Paraguay, the ultimate underdogs at +1700, reached the last 16 the hard way, holding their nerve to beat Germany on penalties.

### Simulation Report — **Paraguay 1–1 France (a.e.t.) — France win 4–3 on penalties**
The heavy favourites survived the fright of their tournament. France had the ball less than they would have liked (48%) and yet piled up **27 shots to Paraguay's 17**, **Kylian Mbappé** (ten attempts) and **Ousmane Dembélé** (nine) hammering away at a packed Paraguayan block. But it was the underdogs who struck first: on **70'**, **Julio Enciso** — a relentless outlet who fired eleven shots of his own — punished a rare opening to stun the Linc. France needed just four minutes to respond, **Mbappé** levelling on **74'** to force the game the champions' way. Neither side could find a winner in normal time or across thirty minutes of extra time, and so, fittingly for Paraguay, it went to penalties.

The shootout was pure theatre. Through five kicks each it was **3–3** — Enciso, Ávalos and Almirón scoring for Paraguay; Mbappé, Olise and Tchouaméni for France; **Canale**, the R32 hero, this time denied, with Dembélé and Rabiot also missing for France. Into sudden death: **Diego Gómez** and then **Cubas** could not convert for Paraguay, and when **Bradley Barcola** stepped up and buried his, France had it — **4–3**, and a place in the quarter-finals clung to by their fingernails.

**AI takeaway:** The machine's reading is of a mismatch on paper that the pitch refused to honour. France dominated the shot count and eventually the scoreboard tiebreak, but Paraguay's deep, disciplined 4-4-2 — Cubas screening, Enciso and Almirón springing the counter — turned a −550 favourite into a 120-minute grind and a coin-flip shootout. Mbappé's equaliser and Barcola's decisive kick spared France the tournament's biggest shock; Alfaro's side, beaten only from twelve yards after taking the world's form team the distance, leave with enormous credit. The favourites advance — but on this evidence the gap to the outsiders is far narrower than the odds believe.
