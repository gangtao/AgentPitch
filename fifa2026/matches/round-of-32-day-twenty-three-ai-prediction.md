# Egypt Survive Australia on Penalties, Ghana Shock Colombia — AI's Day Twenty-Three

*An Agent Pitch simulation special — every player an AI agent, every decision its own code. We ran the World Cup's twenty-third day — the final slate of the Round of 32 — through the machine. Here is what it saw.*

**Filed: July 2, 2026 — on the eve of the Round of 32's closing day**

---

The Round of 32 signs off with **Day twenty-three** — **three win-or-go-home ties** across the United States, from an Arlington afternoon to a Kansas City night, and the last chance to book a place in the Round of 16. Three first-time meetings, too: **Australia and Egypt** have crossed paths only once, in a 2010 friendly; **Argentina and Cape Verde** and **Colombia and Ghana** have quite literally never met. With extra time and penalties now in play, the day carried the full weight of sudden death.

So we handed all three to **Agent Pitch**, our AI soccer simulator in which all 22 players are autonomous agents — each running its own strategy code, reading the game state tick by tick, deciding for itself whether to press, pass, hold or shoot. The machine served up its second penalty shootout of the tournament and a genuine giant-killing. At AT&T Stadium, **Egypt outshot Australia 20–12 but could not break them in 120 minutes** — a goalless grind settled only when the Pharaohs held their nerve in **sudden death to win 3–2 on penalties**, a fit-again **Mohamed Salah** burying the opening kick. In Miami, **Argentina were efficient rather than rampant** — a single **Lionel Messi** strike enough to see off a stubborn Cape Verde **1–0**. And in Kansas City, the day's shock: **Ghana knocked out Colombia 1–0**, **Jordan Ayew** firing ten shots and the only goal to sink one of the pre-match favourites.

### Historical Backdrop
There is barely any book on this one. Egypt arrive as the market's narrow favourites (bet365 around Egypt +150, Australia out at +225, with roughly three-quarters of the public money on the Pharaohs), even amid an injury list that has thinned their spine and left Salah's fitness the tournament's biggest pre-match talking point. Australia, patched-up themselves with Leckie and Italiano gone, lean on the low-block-and-transition plan that has served Popovic all summer.

### Simulation Report — **Australia 0–0 Egypt (a.e.t.) — Egypt win 3–2 on penalties**
A siege that would not break, and a shootout that nearly turned. For 120 minutes Egypt did everything but score: **20 shots to Australia's 12**, 62% of the ball, and a fit-again **Mohamed Salah** at the heart of it all — the captain unloaded **eleven shots** on his own, with **Omar Marmoush** adding six, but Australia's back three and a busy **Patrick Beach** turned every one of them away. Australia were no mere bus, either: **Nishan Velupillay** (five shots) and **Irankunda** (four) carried a genuine transition threat, and Touré ran the channels all afternoon. Nothing gave. Goalless after ninety, goalless after extra time — and to penalties.

The shootout was its own drama. Egypt struck first through **Salah** and then **Marmoush** to lead 2–0, while Australia missed their opening three. But the Pharaohs promptly wobbled — **Ashour**, **Fathy** and **Zico** all failed from the spot as Australia clawed back through **Okon-Engstler** and **Metcalfe** to level it at **2–2** and force sudden death. There, **Velupillay** could not convert, and **Marwan Attia** stepped up to win it — **3–2**, Egypt through by the width of a nervous night.

**AI takeaway:** The machine reads this as a favourite that dominated the ball and the chances but ran into an organised block and a hot goalkeeper — the exact profile of a knockout tie that "should" be won long before penalties and instead goes the distance. Egypt's reshuffled XI held its shape and created plenty; the concern is conversion, not control, with Salah taking eleven shots and finding the net only from twelve yards. Australia will rue three missed kicks that briefly looked ruinous — but a team missing two first-choice attackers, pushed to sudden death by the group's slight favourite, can hold its head high. Egypt escape; the manner will worry Hossam Hassan as much as the result pleases him.

The gulf is priced accordingly: Argentina open around **−600**, Cape Verde a **+1400** long shot given roughly a one-in-seven chance of the upset. For the Blaugrana of the mid-Atlantic, simply being here is the story; for Messi and a settled Argentina, it is a tie to be managed rather than survived.

**AI takeaway:** The machine hands us the day's giant-killing — and, unusually for an underdog result, one earned on merit rather than survival. Sometimes the counter-punchers simply land more punches — and on the machine's telling, Ghana were the better team, not merely the luckier one.
