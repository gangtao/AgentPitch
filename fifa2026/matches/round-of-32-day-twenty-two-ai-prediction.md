# Spain and Portugal Cruise, Algeria Stun Switzerland in Extra Time — AI's Day Twenty-Two

*An Agent Pitch simulation special — every player an AI agent, every decision its own code. We ran the World Cup's twenty-second day — the Round of 32 rolling on, now with extra time and penalties in play — through the machine. Here is what it saw.*

**Filed: July 1, 2026 — on the eve of the knockouts' sixth day**

---

The Round of 32 keeps its three-a-day rhythm into **Day twenty-two** — **three win-or-go-home ties** strung coast to coast, from a SoFi Stadium afternoon in Los Angeles to a Vancouver night. And this is the day the knockouts get their teeth: for the first time we let the machine play **true sudden death** — if a tie is level after ninety minutes, it plays **extra time**, and if still level, a **penalty shootout**. Reigning European champions **Spain** meet a **Austria** side at its first World Cup since 1998; **Portugal** and **Croatia** renew an old knockout acquaintance in Toronto; and in Vancouver, **Switzerland** and **Algeria** meet for the first time in a competitive match.

So we handed all three to **Agent Pitch**, our AI soccer simulator in which all 22 players are autonomous agents — each running its own strategy code, reading the game state tick by tick, deciding for itself whether to press, pass, hold or shoot. The machine made it a day of favourites and a fright that turned into a fall. At SoFi, **Spain were efficient rather than emphatic** — two first-half goals through **Álex Baena** and **Mikel Oyarzabal** settling a tie in which a game Austria actually matched them for shots. In Toronto, **Portugal's young forwards did the damage** — **João Félix** and **Pedro Neto** striking inside four second-half minutes to sink Croatia **2–0**, Cristiano Ronaldo firing six blanks around them while Luka Modrić's side managed just four shots all night. And in Vancouver, the night belonged to the underdog: **Algeria stunned Switzerland 2–1 after extra time**, **Amine Gouiri**'s 119th-minute winner completing an upset that the new extra-time rule made possible.

**Austria**, under **Ralf Rangnick**, set up in a compact, high-pressing **4-2-3-1**. **Alexander Schlager** in goal; a back four of **Phillipp Mwene**, captain **David Alaba**, **Philipp Lienhart** and **Stefan Posch**; a **Nicolas Seiwald**–**Xaver Schlager** double pivot; **Romano Schmid**, **Marcel Sabitzer** and **Konrad Laimer** behind lone striker **Marko Arnautović**. ### Historical Backdrop

Austria's proudest result remains the oldest: their **2–1 win at the 1978 World Cup**, the only time these sides have met at a finals. Austria have not beaten Spain since a 1990 friendly, and the two have not played at all in over fifteen years. Now Austria arrive at their **first World Cup since 1998** to face the **reigning European champions**, who swept their group without conceding — the bookmakers make Spain prohibitive favourites, around −340 with Austria out beyond +1000.

### Simulation Report — **Spain 2–0 Austria**
Efficient, not emphatic — and a scoreline that flattered the gap. Spain struck early: on **4'**, **Álex Baena** finished off a patient move to quiet SoFi's neutral majority, and on **29'** **Mikel Oyarzabal** doubled it before the break. That, it turned out, was the game — because Austria refused to be the parked bus the odds implied. Rangnick's side pressed, broke, and **matched Spain shot-for-shot: 10 attempts to Spain's 11**, with **Konrad Laimer** the busiest man on the pitch (eight shots) driving in off the right. What they lacked was the final touch: Arnautović was starved of clean service, and Alexander Schlager's goal survived the flurries. **Lamine Yamal** (seven shots) probed all night for Spain but it was the two first-half strikes that did the job. The champions march on — made to work far harder than a two-goal win suggests.

**AI takeaway:** The machine reads this as a favourite winning on ruthlessness, not dominance. Spain took their two good moments and controlled the rest; possession split evenly at 50%, and Austria genuinely tested them — ten shots and a hyperactive Laimer are not the numbers of a team that came to survive. De la Fuente will be delighted with the clean sheet and the two early goals, less so with a midfield that never truly took the game by the throat. For Austria, elimination will sting precisely because they were competitive — Rangnick's press did its job everywhere except the one box that mattered. Spain are through, but this was a warning that the closing-out will get harder.

## Match 2 — Portugal vs Croatia

### Historical Backdrop
These sides know each other well: **10 meetings, Portugal leading 7–1 with two draws**. They first met at **Euro 1996** (Portugal 3–0, Luís Figo scoring inside four minutes), and Croatia's **only** win in the fixture is fresh: a **2–1 in June 2024** on Portuguese soil, their most recent meeting. The subplot writes itself — **Ronaldo, 41, and Modrić, 40**, former Real Madrid teammates chasing one last deep run. Portugal carry the sharper attack and the market's confidence (around −290, with roughly three-quarters of the public backing them); Croatia bring the resilience that has carried them to a final and a semi in the last two World Cups.

Croatia, so often the great survivors, could barely muster a threat: **Modrić, Budimir and Baturina had one shot apiece** and Diogo Costa was largely a spectator. A comfortable, controlled Portuguese win.

**AI takeaway:** The machine's story is a generational passing of the baton. Twenty shots to four is a hammering in everything but the final margin. For Croatia, the concern is stark: a midfield of Modrić, Kovačić and Sučić that could not string chances together, and a night when their knockout pedigree counted for nothing. Portugal look like a team peaking at the right time; Croatia look, at last, their age.
