### Simulation Report — **South Africa 1–3 Canada**
A heist in the Los Angeles sun. South Africa played the better football — **58% possession and 21 shots to Canada's 14** — and struck first, **Thapelo Maseko** finishing on **13'** to give Bafana the lead their early control deserved. But Canada are built precisely to win games like this. On **32'**, captain **Jonathan David** — relentless all afternoon, with **ten** of his side's attempts — levelled it, and after the break Marsch's transition machine took over: **Ali Ahmed** on **51'** and **Nathan Saliba** on **64'** turned the game on its head for **3–1**. South Africa kept coming — **Lyle Foster** and **Oswin Appollis** had six shots apiece, **Mofokeng** four — but found Crépeau and a back line that bent without breaking, and every Canadian counter seemed to end with a shot on Williams's goal. Bafana's best was not enough; Canada's cutting edge was decisive.

**AI takeaway:** The machine reads the oldest knockout story of all — the side that dominates the ball going out to the side that takes its chances. South Africa's 21 shots, spread sensibly across Foster, Appollis, Maseko and Mofokeng, are the image of a team that did almost everything but finish; David's ten-shot, one-goal afternoon and the clinical second-half strikes from Ahmed and Saliba are why Canada move on. Two first-time knockout sides, and it is the co-hosts — even exiled from home — who advance to the last 16. For South Africa, a maiden knockout run ends with pride intact and the sense that the margins, not the performance, beat them.
