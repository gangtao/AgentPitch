### Simulation Report — **France 1–2 Spain**
The game the machine played is the game everyone expects: **Spain with the ball (59% possession), France with the counter** — and Spain landed the first blow before the plan had a chance to breathe. **8'**, Oyarzabal: Spain's opening spell of pressure ended with the captain-in-waiting of clinical finishing taking his one moment of the entire evening — **one shot all match, one goal**. From there the pattern locked in. France sat in the 4-4-1-1 and broke in waves through Dembélé, who fired **nine shots** — more than any player on either side — at a Spanish rest-defence that bent and never quite broke. The halves were eerily symmetrical: seven shots per team in each, **14–14 overall**, a genuine heavyweight exchange rather than a siege.

The knockout punch came within a quarter-hour of the restart, and it belonged to the man whose goal in Munich still follows this fixture around. **58'**, **Lamine Yamal** — six shots on the night, drifting off the right into the half-space he has made his own — finally beat Maignan to make it 0–2. France responded the way six straight wins said they would: **Michael Olise, 77'**, cutting in from the right to score with — remarkably, mirroring Oyarzabal — **his only shot of the match**. But the equalizer never came. Spain's midfield (44 tackle contests, 22 won, to France's 39 and 17) strangled the last fifteen minutes the way they strangled the Euro 2024 semi-final: possession as defence, Rodri and Fabián Ruiz passing France's press into the Texas turf. Mbappé, the man the French gameplan was built around, was held to **three shots**; Baena matched Yamal's six at the other end. No extra time required.

**AI takeaway:** The machine sees a rivalry that has found its pattern: Spain keep the ball, France keep their shape, and the difference is which side's economist cashes first. The shot ledger was dead level at 14, but France's volume ran through Dembélé (nine attempts, none in) while Spain's ran through three different scorers-in-waiting — and the two most efficient men on the pitch, Oyarzabal and Olise, each scored with their only attempt. That France's goal came from Olise and not Mbappé (three shots, quiet by his standards) is the detail Deschamps will hate most: erase the superstar and France still create, but not quite enough. Spain's reward, per the machine, is a July 19 final in New Jersey — their record against this simulation's other predicted finalist can wait until Day 33 settles who that is.
