# Argentina — Tactical Profile

## Identity & Philosophy
Reigning World Cup champions (2022) and back-to-back Copa América winners (2021, 2024), Argentina under Lionel Scaloni are the most complete side of their generation: pragmatic but technical, comfortable in possession yet ruthless on the break. Scaloni's philosophy fuses Bielsa-era verticality with Simeone-era defensive grit, all built around giving Lionel Messi total freedom in the final third. The knockout run has hardened the team: the possession-first 4-3-3 of the group stage gave way to a battle-tested **4-4-2** with a double pivot — and the same eleven has now started and won two straight knockout rounds. This is a settled champion peaking at the right time, one win from a third straight final appearance across 2014/2022 lineage.

**Group stage (Group J): won it.** Argentina opened against Algeria, cruised through with six early points and closed with a 2-0 win over Austria. Messi seized the all-time World Cup scoring record along the way.

**Round of 32 — Argentina 3-2 Cabo Verde (AET, Fri 3 Jul, Miami).** The near-death experience: only Diney Borges' 111th-minute own goal spared the champions the biggest upset in tournament history after Lisandro Martínez's 92nd-minute goal had been cancelled out. 120 draining minutes in the Miami heat.

**Round of 16 — Argentina 3-2 Egypt (Tue 7 Jul, Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Atlanta).** From 0-2 down with ten minutes of normal time left: Romero's header, Messi's smashed 83rd-minute equaliser, and Enzo Fernández's stoppage-time winning header. Argentina have already won at the semi-final venue.

**Quarter-final — Argentina 3-1 Switzerland (AET, Sat 11 Jul, Arrowhead Stadium, Kansas City).** The cleanest knockout performance yet. Mac Allister headed in Messi's 10th-minute corner; Dan Ndoye's curled finish levelled it in the 67th, and Breel Embolo's second yellow (simulation, 72') left the Swiss with ten. The block held until extra time, when Álvarez finally broke his drought with the 112th-minute winner and substitute Lautaro Martínez sealed it at 120+1. Messi's scoring streak ended, but the corner assist made the opener and he still leads the Golden Boot. Scaloni managed legs well: Tagliafico (78'), De Paul and Molina (85'), Enzo (90'), Romero (105') and Paredes (110') were all withdrawn before the finish.

**Semi-final — England (Wed 15 Jul, Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Atlanta).** Same XI expected: **Emi Martínez; Molina, Romero, Lisandro Martínez, Tagliafico; De Paul, Paredes, Enzo Fernández, Mac Allister; Messi, Álvarez** (4-4-2). Availability is perfect: no Argentine is injured, no red or second-yellow was picked up in Kansas City, and FIFA wiped all single yellow cards after the quarter-finals — the disciplinary slate is clean again, so nobody plays inhibited. Fatigue is a wash: England also needed 120 minutes to beat Norway 2-1 in Miami the same night (a Bellingham brace after Schjelderup's opener), so both sides arrive off extra time with three full days' rest.

- Role mapping (roster order in `argentina.yaml`):

  - index 1: LB — **Nicolás Tagliafico** (#3) — disciplined 2022-final veteran, withdrawn on 78' in KC so arrives fresher than most. His flank is the red zone of this tie: England's right — Saka or Madueke — attacks it, so overlaps are selective and Mac Allister doubles back with him.
  - index 2: LCB — **Lisandro Martínez** (#6) — combative, intelligent left-footed ball-playing CB; strong in the tackle, steps out to intercept, distributes crisply. Scored vs Cape Verde — a set-piece threat too. Went the full 120 in KC.
  - index 3: RCB — **Cristian Romero** (#13) — front-foot, aggressive stepper; rested from 105' in KC to protect the knee he has managed since June. Knows Harry Kane's game inside-out from their Tottenham years together — he is the designated tracker when Kane drops deep.
  - index 4: RB — **Nahuel Molina** (#26) — provides all the width on the right because Messi drifts inside; withdrawn on 85' in KC. His recovery pace covers England's left-side counters.
  - index 5: LM — **Alexis Mac Allister** (#20) — left-sided midfielder who tucks inside; scored the opener vs Switzerland (a header from Messi's corner) and remains the prime second-phase set-piece threat. Played 120 — but he is the squad's iron lung.
  - index 6: LCM — **Enzo Fernández** (#24) — box-to-box playmaker who advances into the left half-space when Paredes drops; long diagonals, split passes, and late arrivals in the box (the Egypt winner). Off on 90' in KC. His duel with Bellingham between the lines may decide the tie.
  - index 7: RCM/regista — **Leandro Paredes** (#5) — the deep metronome; dictates tempo with long switches, screens the back four, and is Argentina's most reliable penalty taker.   - index 8: RM — **Rodrigo De Paul** (#7) — Messi's bodyguard, box-to-box engine, all-action presser; off on 85' in KC so arrives with fuel. Shuttles to cover when Molina overlaps and picks up Bellingham's drifts to England's left.
  - index 9: ST — **Julián Álvarez** (#9) — the drought is over: the 112th-minute winner vs Switzerland was his release. Preferred over Lautaro for mobility and pressing; stretches defences with channel runs and opens the pockets Messi lives in. Leads the press on England's build-up.
  - index 10: CF/free role — **Lionel Messi** (#10) — floats off Álvarez, drifting right, between the lines, wherever the game is. No defensive responsibility. 8 goals — still leading the Golden Boot over Kane (6) — and the all-time World Cup top scorer. The scoring streak ended in KC but the corner assist won the game's first act. At 39, after two 120-minute nights, every sprint is budgeted more tightly than ever. In-match penalties remain Paredes' (Messi has missed two this tournament).

## Style of Play
### Build-up
Patient short build-up from the back: Emi Martínez splits the CBs, Paredes drops between them to form a 3+1, fullbacks push high for width. Progression runs through Paredes' long switches and Enzo stepping into the left half-space. When pressed, Argentina goes long to Álvarez running the channel rather than to a target man — second balls are hunted by De Paul and Mac Allister. England under Tuchel press higher than Switzerland did: expect Kane curving onto Paredes and Bellingham jumping to a CB. The answer is the third-man bounce — CB to De Paul, first-time into Messi's feet — and the early ball over Tagliafico's side into Álvarez when England's line steps up.

### Pressing
**Mid-block** primarily, with selective high-press triggers: a back-pass to Pickford, or a CB taking a heavy first touch. Álvarez leads the press — curving runs to cut the lane to Declan Rice, England's build-up anchor. Messi does NOT press; he hovers between the lines, conserving energy for the transition. De Paul and Mac Allister jump into midfield duels. Rest-defence discipline is paramount: England's counters through Saka/Madueke and Bellingham's carries are the fastest Argentina have faced this tournament — Paredes plus both CBs stay anchored at all times.

### Defensive shape
A genuine flat **4-4-2**: Mac Allister and De Paul tuck into the midfield four alongside Enzo and Paredes; Messi walks alongside Álvarez up top. The two England problems to solve: (1) **Kane dropping deep** — Romero follows him into midfield (he knows Kane from Spurs), with Lisandro sliding across and Paredes filling the gap Kane vacates behind; (2) **Bellingham's late runs beyond Kane** — a brace vs Norway, both arriving from deep — which belong to Paredes and the near-side pivot, never to a CB stepping out. Wide midfielders double on Saka/Madueke and Rashford's side; the high line is used sparingly — England's route in behind is too fast to bait.

### Wide play
Asymmetric: **right** = Molina's aggressive overlap with De Paul covering, feeding the Messi-Molina-De Paul triangle where most chances are manufactured — and where England's weaker defensive flank lives if Guehi's injury forces a reshuffled back line; **left** = Tagliafico's selective overlap with Mac Allister drifting inside and Enzo underlapping — kept conservative because Saka/Madueke counter into that space. Fatigue caveat: three straight extra-time-or-emotional knockouts means fullbacks pick their sprints — controlled bursts, not waves.

### Final third
Patterns: Messi dropping between the lines to slip Álvarez in behind; Messi-to-Molina cut-back from the right byline; Enzo's late arrival in the box (the Egypt winner); Mac Allister attacking the back post (the Switzerland opener); Paredes' switches to isolate a full-back. England will not sit as deep as Switzerland — the space behind their line is the prize, and Álvarez's channel runs plus one flash of Messi is the template. If the game opens up late, Lautaro Martínez off the bench has scored in extra time and is the fresh-legs hammer.

## Set Pieces
- Attacking corners: Messi delivers from the right (in-swinger) — his corner made Mac Allister's opener vs Switzerland — Mac Allister from the left (in-swinger). Primary aerial targets: Romero, Lisandro Martínez, Enzo Fernández arriving late (the Egypt winner), and now Mac Allister himself at the back post. Paredes loiters at the edge for rebounds and volleys. England defend set pieces well zonally — early deliveries to the near-post seam are the plan.
- Defending corners: **hybrid** — three zonal markers on the six-yard line, four man-markers, two short-corner blockers. Romero attacks the first ball. England are elite from dead balls (Kane, Stones, Bellingham on the move) — this is the tie's biggest single danger. Keep Álvarez high as the counter outlet.
- Free kicks: Messi takes direct from any zone within 30 yards — non-negotiable. Paredes and Enzo deliver wide free kicks into the box. Beware conceding free kicks in wide areas: England's whipped deliveries are their cleanest route to goal.
- Penalties (in match): **Paredes** primary — Messi has missed two this tournament (vs Austria, wide; vs Egypt, saved) and has handed over spot-kick duty. **Mac Allister** secondary, **Álvarez** tertiary.
- **Penalty-shootout order (England's shootout scars vs Argentina run to 1998):**
  1. **Paredes** — ice-cold, scored in the 2022 final shootout (penalty 16).
  2. **Messi** — still steps up first-five in a shootout despite the in-match misses (penalty 14).
  3. **Mac Allister** — composed, high technique (penalty 15).
  4. **Álvarez** — full of confidence after the KC winner (penalty 15).
  5. **Enzo Fernández** — penalty 15.
  - Behind them all: **Emi Martínez** (#23), the 2022 shootout hero who has saved English penalties for years in the Premier League — Argentina's decisive edge from twelve yards against Pickford, himself a shootout keeper of pedigree.

## decide() Decision Priorities
Concrete rules the LLM should encode:
1. **If my `player_id` ends with `_10` (Messi, free role) and team_phase == "defending":** Hold or slow-walk toward the halfway line — do NOT track back. Conserve stamina; at 39, after back-to-back 120-minute nights, every sprint is budgeted.
2. **If my `player_id` ends with `_10` (Messi) and I have the ball between the lines:** Prefer the killer Pass — ST `_9` (Álvarez) running in behind England's higher line, or RB `_4` (Molina) overlapping right. Shoot when within 22 units with a lane (shoot 19); do NOT hold the ball under a double-team — release and go again.
3. **If my `role == "GK"` (player_id `_0`, Emi Martínez):** Stay home — sweep only up to 20 units out of the box when a through-ball is certain to be reached first. Distribute short to `_2`/`_3` unless pressed, then long toward `_9`'s channel run over the England press.
4. **If my `player_id` ends with `_7` (Paredes, regista) and team_phase == "attacking":** Drop between/behind the CBs to form the 3+1; recycle with safe forward passes and switch play to the far-side fullback. Never carry into traffic — England's counter-press feeds Bellingham. If no forward option, pass square to a CB — tempo is control.
5. **If my `player_id` ends with `_4` (RB, Molina) and team_phase == "attacking" and the ball is central or left:** Advance high and wide right — the overlap is automatic because `_10` drifts inside. But if the team just lost the ball, sprint back immediately: England counter into the fullback zones faster than anyone left in the tournament.
6. **If my `player_id` ends with `_8` (RM, De Paul) and the ball-carrier's `player_id` ends with `_10` (Messi):** Position 8-12 units behind and to the carrier's right — cover-shadow for the counter-press the moment `_10` loses it; Bellingham lives on exactly those turnovers.
7. **If my `player_id` ends with `_6` (LCM, Enzo) and team_phase == "attacking" and the ball is wide:** Make the late run into the box toward the far post (the Egypt winner) — arrive, don't wait. Otherwise offer the half-space link and hit split passes (pass 18).
8. **If my `player_id` ends with `_5` (LM, Mac Allister) and team_phase == "defending":** Tuck inside to make the flat midfield four and double on England's right winger with `_1` (Tagliafico); when possession is won, release `_9` or find `_10` between the lines before the block re-sets. On attacking corners from the right, attack the back post — the Switzerland opener.
9. **If my `player_id` ends with `_9` (ST, Álvarez) and team_phase == "defending":** Stay high as the front two with `_10` and lead the press on England's deepest midfielder (Rice); do NOT drop into midfield. On transition, run the channels immediately — England's line plays higher than Switzerland's did.
10. **If my `role == "FWD"` or `role == "MID"` and Argentina just lost the ball in the opposition third:** Counter-press — Tackle the nearest opponent within 6 units for 5 seconds; deny the first pass of the England counter before it reaches Bellingham or the wingers. Exception: `_10` does not counter-press.
11. **If team is leading by 1+ goals and minute > 75:** Drop deeper, recycle possession, kill the clock — `_7` (Paredes) dictates, `_8`/`_5` hold the ball in the corners. Argentina has played three heavy knockout matches; do not trade punches late with a team built on late Bellingham goals.
12. **Penalties:** in-match penalty goes to `_7` (Paredes), NOT `_10` — Messi has missed two this tournament. Direct free kicks within 30 units remain `_10`'s (Messi).

## Tournament Mindset
**Semi-final — two wins from back-to-back titles, and the biggest rivalry fixture in World Cup history is the gate.** Argentina v England: Rattín 1966, the Hand of God and the Goal of the Century in 1986, Beckham's red card and the shootout of 1998, Beckham's penalty of 2002 — the first World Cup meeting between them in 24 years, with a final on the line. Scaloni is publicly defusing it — "it's just a football match" — precisely because his squad knows it is not; the instruction is to play the game, not the history.

The opponent is serious: **England under Tuchel** survived Norway 2-1 in extra time in Miami on a Jude Bellingham brace after falling behind, with Kane on 6 goals hunting Messi's Golden Boot, and genuine width in Saka and Madueke (who started the QF). What Scaloni must solve: Bellingham's late runs beyond Kane (both Norway goals came from deep arrivals), Kane's drop-deep link play (Romero's job — he knows him from Spurs), England's elite set-piece delivery, and a counter-attack faster than anything Argentina have faced. What favors Argentina: the venue — Mercedes-Benz Stadium, where the Egypt comeback already happened — a possible England reshuffle in defence (Guehi doubt), identical fatigue (both played 120 minutes on July 11), a wiped-clean card slate, and the tournament's best player still deciding matches.

The plan: start fast — Argentina's slow starts have burned two lives already, and England punish deficits chased. Control through Paredes, strangle the Bellingham pocket, make Rice defend facing his own goal, and let Messi and a confident Álvarez work England's line. Hold Lautaro — extra-time scorer in KC — Nico González and the fresh legs for the final act. And if it reaches twelve yards, Argentina hold the trump cards England fear most: a rebuilt shootout order around Paredes, Messi second, and Emiliano Martínez — England's personal penalty tormentor — in goal. Win the game, not the grudge — but win.
