# Argentina — Tactical Profile

## Identity & Philosophy
Reigning World Cup champions (2022) and back-to-back Copa América winners (2021, 2024), Argentina under Lionel Scaloni are the most complete side of their generation: pragmatic but technical, comfortable in possession yet ruthless on the break. Scaloni's philosophy fuses Bielsa-era verticality with Simeone-era defensive grit, all built around giving Lionel Messi total freedom in the final third. But the knockouts have exposed the champions' mortality — twice they have stared elimination in the face, and twice the old champion instincts saved them. For the quarter-final, Scaloni has hardened the team: the possession-first 4-3-3 of the group stage has given way to a battle-tested **4-4-2** with a double pivot, Julián Álvarez's running up front, and Messi as the free man.

**Group stage (Group J): won it.** Argentina opened against Algeria, cruised through with six early points and had the group sewn up with a game to spare, closing with a 2-0 win over Austria in which the only blemish was Messi firing a penalty wide. Messi seized the all-time World Cup scoring record along the way.

**Round of 32 — Argentina 3-2 Cabo Verde (AET, Fri 3 Jul, Miami).** The near-death experience. Messi's 29th-minute breakaway opener was cancelled by Deroy Duarte; Lisandro Martínez restored the lead in the 92nd, Sidny Lopes Cabral curled in an outrageous 103rd-minute equaliser, and only Diney Borges' 111th-minute own goal spared the champions the biggest upset in tournament history. 120 draining minutes in the Miami heat.

**Round of 16 — Argentina 3-2 Egypt (Tue 7 Jul, Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Atlanta).** One of the great World Cup comebacks. Scaloni made three changes (Tagliafico, Paredes and Álvarez in; Medina, Almada and Lautaro out) — and it nearly backfired: Yasser Ibrahim headed Egypt ahead, Mostafa Shobeir saved Messi's 21st-minute penalty, and Mostafa Zico made it 2-0. Then the storm: Cristian Romero's header, Messi's smashed 83rd-minute equaliser — his 8th goal of the tournament, most of anyone — and **Enzo Fernández's stoppage-time winning header**. From 0-2 to 3-2 in the final quarter of the match.

**Quarter-final — Switzerland (Sat 11 Jul, GEHA Field at Arrowhead Stadium, Kansas City).** Scaloni is expected to keep the XI that finished the Egypt comeback: **Emi Martínez; Molina, Romero, Lisandro Martínez, Tagliafico; De Paul, Paredes, Enzo Fernández, Mac Allister; Messi, Álvarez** (4-4-2). No Argentine is injured or suspended, and the disciplinary slate is clean — knockout yellows were wiped before the QF and no Argentina player carries a caution, so nobody is one booking from a semi-final ban. The worry is legs, not availability: 120 minutes vs Cape Verde plus the emotional Egypt comeback is a heavy toll, and Messi is 39. Switzerland, in their first quarter-final since 1954, arrive off a 0-0, 4-3-on-penalties grind past Colombia — organised, physical, and happy to make this ugly.

## Formation
- Shape: **4-4-2** (flat two banks out of possession; in possession Paredes drops into a 4-1-3-2 diamond with Enzo advancing and Messi roaming off Álvarez)
- Role mapping (roster order in `argentina.yaml`):
  - index 0: GK — **Emiliano Martínez** (#23) — elite shot-stopper, vocal organizer, mentality monster on penalties. Stays in his box and dominates aerially; in a knockout tie he is the ultimate shootout insurance — and against a Switzerland side that just won a shootout, that edge matters.
  - index 1: LB — **Nicolás Tagliafico** (#3) — recovered from the calf injury that cost him the group stage and restored for the knockouts. Experienced, disciplined, positionally reliable; overlaps selectively and tucks in to make a back three when Molina bombs on. The 2022 final veteran Scaloni trusts in games like this.
  - index 2: LCB — **Lisandro Martínez** (#6) — combative, intelligent left-footed ball-playing CB; strong in the tackle, steps out to intercept, distributes crisply. Scored the extra-time goal vs Cape Verde — a threat on set pieces too.
  - index 3: RCB — **Cristian Romero** (#13) — front-foot, aggressive stepper, will sprint 20 yards to crunch a forward; biggest defensive risk-taker. His header started the Egypt comeback.
  - index 4: RB — **Nahuel Molina** (#26) — quicker and more attacking than Montiel, preferred for the knockouts; provides all the width on the right because Messi drifts inside. His pace also covers Switzerland's counters down that flank.
  - index 5: LM — **Alexis Mac Allister** (#20) — left-sided midfielder who tucks inside; late runs into the box, second-phase set-piece threat, presses relentlessly. The connective tissue between Tagliafico's flank and the front two.
  - index 6: LCM — **Enzo Fernández** (#24) — box-to-box playmaker who advances into the left half-space when Paredes drops; long diagonals, split passes — and now a match-winner: his stoppage-time header beat Egypt. Arrives late in the box more than ever.
  - index 7: RCM/regista — **Leandro Paredes** (#5) — the deep metronome, drafted in for the knockouts to add control and physicality. Dictates tempo with long switches, screens the back four, and is Argentina's most reliable penalty taker. Card-prone — must pick his fouls carefully.
  - index 8: RM — **Rodrigo De Paul** (#7) — Messi's bodyguard, box-to-box engine, all-action presser on the right of midfield. Covers every blade of grass Messi doesn't; shuttles to cover when Molina overlaps.
  - index 9: ST — **Julián Álvarez** (#9) — preferred over Lautaro for his mobility and pressing; stretches defences with channel runs and opens the pockets Messi lives in. Honest, tireless — though his finishing has been off the boil all tournament.
  - index 10: CF/free role — **Lionel Messi** (#10) — floats off Álvarez, drifting right, between the lines, wherever the game is. No defensive responsibility. 8 goals — leading the Golden Boot — and the all-time World Cup top scorer. At 39 his legs are managed but the decisive moments are still his. Caveat: he has missed two penalties this tournament (a World Cup first), so in-match spot kicks now go to Paredes.

## Style of Play
### Build-up
Patient short build-up from the back: Emi Martínez splits the CBs, Paredes drops between them to form a 3+1, fullbacks push high for width. Progression runs through Paredes' long switches and Enzo stepping into the left half-space. When pressed, Argentina goes long to Álvarez running the channel rather than to a target man — second balls are hunted by De Paul and Mac Allister. Against a compact Swiss 4-2-3-1 mid-block marshalled by Xhaka, expect Argentina to dominate the ball and probe patiently; the double pivot (Paredes + one of Enzo/Mac Allister) stays home so a turnover cannot spring Embolo and Vargas into space.

### Pressing
**Mid-block** primarily, with selective high-press triggers: a back-pass to Kobel, or a CB taking a heavy first touch. Álvarez leads the press — his work rate is why he starts over Lautaro — curving runs to cut the lane to Xhaka, Switzerland's build-up brain. Messi does NOT press; he hovers between the lines, conserving energy for the transition. De Paul and Mac Allister jump into midfield duels. Rest-defence discipline is paramount after two knockout scares: Paredes plus both CBs stay anchored at all times.

### Defensive shape
A genuine flat **4-4-2**: Mac Allister and De Paul tuck into the midfield four alongside Enzo and Paredes; Messi walks alongside Álvarez up top. Compact between the lines — Switzerland's route is crosses and set pieces, so the CBs prioritise the box over the high line, and the offside trap is used more sparingly than in the group stage. Wide midfielders double on Switzerland's wingers; Xhaka's diagonals to the far post are the pattern to kill at source by pressing him on reception.

### Wide play
Asymmetric: **right** = Molina's aggressive overlap with De Paul covering, feeding the Messi-Molina-De Paul triangle where most chances are manufactured; **left** = Tagliafico's selective overlap with Mac Allister drifting inside and Enzo underlapping. Fatigue caveat: after 120 minutes in Miami and the Egypt emotional rollercoaster, both fullbacks pick their sprints — this is a team playing in controlled bursts, not waves.

### Final third
Patterns: Messi dropping between the lines to slip Álvarez in behind; Messi-to-Molina cut-back from the right byline; Enzo's late arrival in the box (the Egypt winner); Mac Allister attacking the back post; Paredes' switches to isolate a full-back. Against a deep Swiss block, patience plus one flash of Messi is the template — and if Switzerland over-commit, Álvarez's pace makes Argentina a counter-attack monster.

## Set Pieces
- Attacking corners: Messi delivers from the right (in-swinger), Mac Allister from the left (in-swinger). Primary aerial targets: Romero, Lisandro Martínez (a goalscorer vs Cape Verde), and now Enzo Fernández arriving late (the Egypt winner was a header). Paredes loiters at the edge of the box for rebounds and volleys.
- Defending corners: **hybrid** — three zonal markers on the six-yard line, four man-markers, two short-corner blockers. Romero attacks the first ball. Switzerland are dangerous from dead balls (Embolo, the CBs), so this is a red-zone duty. Keep Álvarez high as the counter outlet.
- Free kicks: Messi takes direct from any zone within 30 yards — this remains non-negotiable. Paredes and Enzo deliver wide free kicks into the box.
- Penalties (in match): **Paredes** primary — Messi has missed two this tournament (vs Austria, wide; vs Egypt, saved by Shobeir — the first player ever to miss two at a single World Cup) and has handed over spot-kick duty. **Mac Allister** secondary, **Álvarez** tertiary.
- **Penalty-shootout order (live — Switzerland just won one, Argentina won two in 2022):**
  1. **Paredes** — ice-cold, scored in the 2022 final shootout (penalty 16).
  2. **Messi** — still steps up first-five in a shootout despite the in-match misses (penalty 14).
  3. **Mac Allister** — composed, high technique (penalty 15).
  4. **Álvarez** — penalty 15.
  5. **Enzo Fernández** — penalty 15.
  - Behind them all: **Emi Martínez** (#23), the 2022 shootout hero — Argentina's decisive edge from twelve yards against Kobel, who just out-dueled Colombia.

## decide() Decision Priorities
Concrete rules the LLM should encode:
1. **If my `player_id` ends with `_10` (Messi, free role) and team_phase == "defending":** Hold or slow-walk toward the halfway line — do NOT track back. Conserve stamina; at 39 every sprint is budgeted.
2. **If my `player_id` ends with `_10` (Messi) and I have the ball between the lines:** Prefer the killer Pass — ST `_9` (Álvarez) running in behind, or RB `_4` (Molina) overlapping right. Shoot when within 22 units with a lane (shoot 19); do NOT hold the ball under a double-team — release and go again.
3. **If my `role == "GK"` (player_id `_0`, Emi Martínez):** Stay home — sweep only up to 20 units out of the box when a through-ball is certain to be reached first. Distribute short to `_2`/`_3` unless pressed, then long toward `_9`'s channel run.
4. **If my `player_id` ends with `_7` (Paredes, regista) and team_phase == "attacking":** Drop between/behind the CBs to form the 3+1; recycle with safe forward passes and switch play to the far-side fullback. Never carry into traffic. If no forward option, pass square to a CB — tempo is control.
5. **If my `player_id` ends with `_4` (RB, Molina) and team_phase == "attacking" and the ball is central or left:** Advance high and wide right — the overlap is automatic because `_10` drifts inside. But if the team just lost the ball, sprint back immediately: Switzerland counter into the fullback zones.
6. **If my `player_id` ends with `_8` (RM, De Paul) and the ball-carrier's `player_id` ends with `_10` (Messi):** Position 8-12 units behind and to the carrier's right — cover-shadow for the counter-press the moment `_10` loses it.
7. **If my `player_id` ends with `_6` (LCM, Enzo) and team_phase == "attacking" and the ball is wide:** Make the late run into the box toward the far post (the Egypt winner) — arrive, don't wait. Otherwise offer the half-space link and hit split passes (pass 18).
8. **If my `player_id` ends with `_5` (LM, Mac Allister) and team_phase == "defending":** Tuck inside to make the flat midfield four; when possession is won, release `_9` or find `_10` between the lines before the block re-sets.
9. **If my `player_id` ends with `_9` (ST, Álvarez) and team_phase == "defending":** Stay high as the front two with `_10` and lead the press on the opposition's deepest midfielder; do NOT drop into midfield. On transition, run the channels immediately.
10. **If my `role == "FWD"` or `role == "MID"` and Argentina just lost the ball in the opposition third:** Counter-press — Tackle the nearest opponent within 6 units for 5 seconds; deny the first pass of the Swiss counter. Exception: `_10` does not counter-press.
11. **If team is leading by 1+ goals and minute > 75:** Drop deeper, recycle possession, kill the clock — `_7` (Paredes) dictates, `_8`/`_5` hold the ball in the corners. Argentina has burned too much fuel this tournament to chase games late.
12. **Penalties:** in-match penalty goes to `_7` (Paredes), NOT `_10` — Messi has missed two this tournament. Direct free kicks within 30 units remain `_10`'s (Messi).

## Key Player Notes
- **Messi (#10, idx 10):** Free role off the striker. No defensive responsibility — walking is a tactical instruction. 8 goals, Golden Boot leader, all-time World Cup top scorer; scored or created the decisive moments in both knockout escapes. Every direct free kick is his. BUT: two missed penalties this tournament (a World Cup first) — in-match spot kicks now belong to Paredes; Messi still takes in a shootout.
- **Álvarez (#9, idx 9):** Preferred over Lautaro since the Round of 16 for mobility and pressing. Stretches the defence so Messi's pockets open. Finishing off the boil all tournament — his job right now is running, pressing, and the channels; the goals are expected to come.
- **Enzo Fernández (#24, idx 6):** From metronome to match-winner — the stoppage-time header vs Egypt. Now an advanced 8 who arrives late in the box, with Paredes behind him doing the deep dictating.
- **Paredes (#5, idx 7):** The knockout addition. Regista who controls tempo and screens the back four; Argentina's best penalty taker (2022 final shootout scorer) and first in the shootout order. Discipline watch: he is Argentina's most booking-prone starter — a QF yellow means nothing (slate is clean) but a red would be fatal.
- **De Paul (#7, idx 8):** Messi's bodyguard on the right of the midfield four — occupies the space Messi vacates and counter-presses his losses. The engine that makes the free role affordable.
- **Mac Allister (#20, idx 5):** Left midfield, tucks inside; late runs, second balls in the box, left-side corners, and second penalty option in-match.
- **Lisandro Martínez (#6, idx 2):** Left-footed LCB; combative, reads the game, crisp distribution — and scored the extra-time goal vs Cape Verde. Set-piece threat at the near post.
- **Romero (#13, idx 3):** Aggressive RCB whose header ignited the Egypt comeback. Steps out hard; the risk-taker of the pair.
- **Tagliafico (#3, idx 1):** Back from the calf injury and straight into the knockout XI over Medina. Disciplined 2022-final veteran; selective overlaps only.
- **Molina (#26, idx 4):** Quicker than Montiel and preferred for the knockouts; supplies all the right-side width and recovery pace against counters.
- **Emi Martínez (#23, idx 0):** Elite shot-stopper and the shootout ace — if this goes to penalties against a Swiss side that just won one, he is the difference.
- **Bench:** Lautaro Martínez (#22) is the game-changing No. 9 held back — fresh legs and an elite finisher for the final half-hour or extra time; Nico González and Nico Paz add width and creativity; Lo Celso is midfield control; Montiel, Medina, Otamendi and Senesi cover the back line. Almada dropped out of the knockout rotation in Scaloni's reshuffle.

## Tournament Mindset
**Quarter-final — three wins from back-to-back titles, and the champions have already used two lives.** Down 0-2 to Egypt with ten minutes to go: won 3-2. Level with Cape Verde in the 103rd minute of the Round of 32: won 3-2 in extra time. Argentina's aura is intact but the warning lights are blinking — slow starts, a missed Messi penalty in each phase of the tournament, and a squad that has played 120 heavy minutes more than it planned. Scaloni's response is control: the 4-4-2 with Paredes, the press led by Álvarez, and no more chasing games from behind.

The opponent is **Switzerland at Arrowhead Stadium, Kansas City** — in their first quarter-final since they hosted in 1954, and here on merit of pure structure: a 0-0 against Colombia over 120 minutes, then a 4-3 shootout won by Kobel's save and Vargas' final kick, with captain Xhaka orchestrating everything in front of the back four (injury doubts over Manzambi, Jaquez and Aebischer may thin their midfield further). They will sit in a compact block, defend the box, slow the game and drag Argentina toward the lottery they just survived. The plan writes itself: score in normal time. Start fast — do not gift a third straight deficit — move Xhaka side to side until the block cracks, and let Messi decide it while fresh legs (Lautaro, González, Paz) are held back for the final act.

If it goes the distance anyway, Argentina still hold the trump cards: a shootout order rebuilt around Paredes with Messi second, and Emiliano Martínez in goal — the 2022 shootout hero against a Swiss side whose own keeper is their hero. Discipline is a freedom here, not a fear: no Argentine carries a yellow into the QF, so the champions can tackle without a semi-final in the back of their minds. Win ugly if needed — but win in 90.
