# Iraq — Tactical Profile

## Identity & Philosophy
Iraq under **Graham Arnold** (the former Australia head coach hired to professionalise the side) is **direct, physical, and ruthlessly organized** — a team that has finally ended a 40-year World Cup absence, qualifying through the AFC route and into Group I alongside France, Senegal and Norway. Arnold's identity is **Anglo-Saxon work-rate + Iraqi technical flair**: a compact mid-block, hard selective pressing, vertical transitions, and intelligent set-piece routines. The final 26 (announced 1 June 2026) blends an experienced, set-piece-savvy spine with a European-based contingent (Ali Jasim at Como, Al-Hamadi at Luton Town on loan from Ipswich, Al-Ammari at Cracovia, Iqbal at Utrecht, Qasem at Nashville). Captain Jalal Hassan returned in goal for the opener. Iraq is the **physical bully** of the AFC region — they will out-run, out-jump, and out-foul most opponents while remaining tactically disciplined, but they are clearly the lowest-rated side in a brutal group. Arnold has installed a level of professional structure the team has lacked for years.

**Matchday 1 update (16 June, vs Norway — lost 1-4, Boston):** Iraq were beaten 1-4 as Erling Haaland scored a brace; Iraq's only goal came before Aymen Hussein later turned a ball into his own net for Norway's fourth. The actual starting XI was a flat **4-4-2: Jalal Hassan; Hussein Ali, Zaid Tahseen, Akam Hashim, Merchas Doski; Ali Jasim, Zaid Ismael, Amir Al-Ammari, Ibrahim Bayesh; Ali Al-Hamadi, Aymen Hussein.** The big surprise was that **Zidane Iqbal began on the bench** — Arnold preferred the more physical, defensively-diligent Zaid Ismael in central midfield, with Iqbal as an in-game creative lever. **Akam Hashim** (Al-Zawraa) started at CB ahead of Rebin Sulaka, and **Ali Jasim** held a wide-midfield slot. For Matchday 2 vs France (22 June, Lincoln Financial Field, Philadelphia) the projected XI is unchanged from the Norway start: Ali Jasim (withdrawn in the second half with a knock vs Norway) trained fully and is available; Jalal Hassan is expected to retain the gloves despite an unconvincing display, with **Ahmed Basil** the speculated alternative if Arnold rotates the keeper.

## Style of Play

### Build-up
Direct. Iraq builds from the back when uncontested but goes long the moment any press arrives — and against France they will be pressed constantly, so expect mostly long. The default long ball is aimed at Aymen Hussein for a knock-down to Al-Hamadi running off him or Bayesh / Ali Jasim arriving from wide. Iraq does NOT prioritize possession — they prioritize getting the ball forward and getting their shape back.

### Pressing
**Mid-block, hard-running, trigger-based.** Iraq drops to the halfway line and waits. Triggers: opposition GK takes a heavy touch, back-pass under duress, opposition midfielder receives on the half-turn. Aymen Hussein and Al-Hamadi lead the press as a front two with curving runs; Bayesh and Ali Jasim jump from the wings; Al-Ammari is the central duel-winner. The press is hard but selective — Iraq's stamina ratings (mostly 14-15) can't sustain a 90-minute high press, and against France they will spend long spells defending rather than pressing.

### Defensive shape
Compact **4-4-2** dropping to **4-5-1**: Bayesh and Ali Jasim tuck into a flat midfield line alongside Al-Ammari and Zaid Ismael, while Al-Hamadi drops onto the opposition pivot and Aymen Hussein screens the deepest CB. The back four sits 22-25 units off goal and stays narrow to protect the box. Against France Iraq drops to **4-5-1** with Al-Hamadi joining the midfield line and Aymen Hussein alone up top.

### Wide play
**Asymmetric.** Left side: Bayesh cuts inside, Doski overlaps for the natural width. Right side: Ali Jasim carries and drives inside, Hussein Ali stays deep as cover (no overlap). The crosses come from the left (Doski) and from deep set-pieces. Iraq is a **crossing team** when they get behind the opposition fullback — Aymen Hussein attacks the near post.

### Final third
Patterns: long ball to Aymen Hussein's chest, knock-down to Al-Hamadi's run or Bayesh's late arrival, shot from 18 yards. Doski's left-flank cross to Aymen Hussein at the near post. Ali Jasim's carry-and-slip to Al-Hamadi sprinting in behind. Iraq will create few chances against France (3-5 at most) — they must be clinical and rely on Aymen Hussein's aerial finishing and set-pieces.

## Set Pieces
**Iraq is a set-piece-dangerous side** with Hashim and Tahseen as aerial threats in both boxes — likely their single most realistic route to a goal against France.
- Attacking corners: **Bayesh** in-swingers from the right (left foot), out-swingers from the left. Targets: Tahseen (penalty spot), Hashim (back post), Aymen Hussein (near post flick-on).
- Defending corners: man-marking heavy. Tahseen marks the most dangerous opposition striker; Hashim marks the second; zonal markers fill the six-yard box; Jalal Hassan stays on the line.
- Free kicks: **Bayesh** direct from the right half-space and from any angle within 27 yards; Zidane Iqbal takes over central dead-balls when he is on.
- Penalties: **Aymen Hussein** primary, **Bayesh** secondary, **Ali Jasim** tertiary (Iqbal an option when on).

## Tournament Mindset
Iraq has qualified for its first World Cup in 40 years — the entire country is behind this team. Drawn in **Group I** with France, Senegal and Norway, they opened with a chastening **1-4 loss to Norway** and now face **France as heavy underdogs on Matchday 2 (22 June, Philadelphia)**. The plan is unambiguous: a **compact deep block (4-5-1), defend the box, and counter** through Aymen Hussein, Al-Hamadi and Ali Jasim, while hunting a goal from set-pieces. The mentality is **honour the shirt, restore pride after Norway, never embarrass ourselves** — keep the score respectable, take no silly red cards, and frustrate a vastly superior side for as long as possible. The vulnerabilities are obvious: creativity is thin with Iqbal starting on the bench, and stamina (mostly 14-15) means Iraq may hold for an hour before tiring against France's depth. A clean sheet would be a triumph; a set-piece goal, a fairytale.
