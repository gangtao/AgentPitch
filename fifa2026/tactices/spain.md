# Spain — Tactical Profile

## Identity & Philosophy
Luis de la Fuente's Spain is the modern heir to tiki-taka, refreshed with vertical wide threats. The midfield is the team's bloodstream — they dictate tempo, rotate constantly, and starve opponents of the ball. Spain won Euro 2024 with 65% average possession, then mixed that with the most direct wide play in world football. Juego de posición with teeth. They arrived as FIFA No. 2, reigning European champions and a heavy favourite; six games in, they have done nothing to soften that billing. De la Fuente named a squad with no Real Madrid players and handed the captaincy to Rodri, his Ballon d'Or-winning pivot.

**The road so far: six wins-or-draws, zero defeats.** Group H winners unbeaten (0-0 Cape Verde, 4-0 Saudi Arabia, 1-0 Uruguay — Baena the winner), 3-0 over Austria in the Round of 32, a 1-0 Round-of-16 win over Portugal settled by Merino's stoppage-time strike that ended Ronaldo's last World Cup, and a **2-1 quarterfinal win over Belgium at SoFi Stadium (Fri 10 Jul)**: Fabián Ruiz opened, De Ketelaere equalized — **the first goal Spain have conceded all tournament** — and substitute **Mikel Merino won it within two minutes of coming on**, turning in the rebound after Lammens spilled Cubarsí's shot. Merino has now won Spain two knockout rounds off the bench. Ledger: **six games, five clean sheets, eleven scored, one conceded.**

**Semifinal — France (Tue 14 Jul, AT&T Stadium, Arlington).** The Euro 2024 semifinal rematch — Spain won that 2-1 with Yamal's wonder-goal and Olmo's winner — and a rematch of the 2021 Nations League final, which France took 2-1. Team news is stable: **the QF XI is expected to go again**, with Fabián Ruiz having made the shirt his own after replacing Pedri (rested/rotated — bench) for the Belgium game and scoring. Nico Williams remains a bench weapon as he builds fitness from his adductor injury; Yéremy Pino is still working back. **No suspensions: single yellow cards were wiped after the quarterfinals.** Probable XI (4-2-3-1): **Simón; Cucurella, Laporte, Cubarsí, Porro; Fabián Ruiz, Rodri; Baena, Olmo, Yamal; Oyarzabal.**

## Style of Play

### Build-up
- From Unai Simón: short to Laporte or Cubarsí every time. Goal-kick to row 1 unless pressed 4v4 high.
- Rodri drops between CBs only when needed (3-2-5 vs high press); otherwise stays as pivot with Fabián to his left.
- Both FBs step into midfield height; Olmo floats between the lines, Fabián arrives late from deep.
- **Possession target ~65%** — France will concede the ball willingly and sit in a 4-4-1-1 mid-block. Spain accepts U-shape passing (CB-DM-CB-DM) to wait for a vertical lane. Patience IS the plan; the one sin is the loose central giveaway that feeds Mbappé.

### Pressing
- Counter-press (Cruyff/Guardiola school): the moment Spain loses it, the nearest 3 players collapse on the carrier for 6 seconds. **Against France this is survival, not style** — every uncontested loss is a potential Mbappé transition. If not won back in 6 seconds, FOUL-FREE containment and sprint back into shape.
- Trigger high press: France CB receives with back to play, or a square pass between CBs is in flight. Oyarzabal leads, Baena & Yamal curve runs to lock the FBs, Olmo jumps the pivot.

### Defensive shape
- 4-1-4-1 with Rodri shielding (Fabián steps into the line). High line (offside trap) — line ~ 50% of pitch length.
- **The Mbappé rule**: Cubarsí and Laporte NEVER both step to the ball; one always protects the depth run. Porro tucks when Doué comes inside. The high line is Spain's identity AND Spain's risk — France's entire gameplan is the ball over/through it.
- Olmo has moderate defensive responsibility; tracking back is shared by the wingers.

### Wide play
- Asymmetric width: **LEFT** Baena (_8) drifts inside off the touchline to combine in the half-space → Cucurella overlaps OUTSIDE him for width. **RIGHT** Yamal (_10) starts wide, drifts into the half-space to cut inside on his left → Porro overlaps outside for the cutback option.
- Half-space combinations: the right-side Yamal-Olmo-Porro triangle is Spain's most dangerous pattern — Yamal cuts in, Olmo links, Porro overlaps into the space Digne leaves.

### Final third
- Patience first: ~30-40 passes per chance is normal. Wait for the FB overlap or the inside-forward cutback.
- Cutback target: Oyarzabal at the penalty spot, Olmo and Fabián arriving late from deep to attack it.
- Yamal's left-foot half-space curler from the right is the recurring termination; Baena's inswinging delivery from the left and Oyarzabal's near-post finish are the alternatives. Fabián's shot from the second wave (see the Belgium opener) is the new wrinkle.

## Tournament Mindset
**Semifinal — two wins from a second consecutive major title.** Six games, eleven scored, ONE conceded — and even that (De Ketelaere's strike for Belgium) was answered within twenty minutes by a bench that keeps winning knockout ties: Merino has now decided the R16 and the QF after coming on. The machine is humming; the finishing that lagged the chance creation against Portugal caught up against Belgium.

The opponent is **France** at AT&T Stadium — the rematch of the Euro 2024 semifinal Spain won 2-1 (Yamal's curler, Olmo's winner) and the 2021 Nations League final France took by the same score. Deschamps' side will not contest possession: expect a compact 4-4-1-1 block, Koné and Rabiot screening, and the entire French gameplan compressed into transition moments for **Mbappé (8 goals)** and **Dembélé (5)**, with Olise's set-piece delivery the third threat. The battle that decides the tie is Spain's rest-defence against France's counter: Rodri + the weak-side CB must erase the Mbappé channel ball before it is even played, the counter-press must strangle the first pass of every French break, and NOBODY dives into a tackle that leaves the line exposed. In possession, move the block side to side until it cracks: the Yamal-Olmo-Porro right-side triangle against Digne's disciplined-but-slower flank is the matchup Spain will mine all night, with Baena-Cucurella combinations stretching the other side.

Spain carry three edges into this tie: the tournament's best defensive record and its control-everything identity; a bench France must fear (Merino's two match-winners, Pedri's tempo, Williams's pace); and the shootout calm if it goes the distance — Oyarzabal, Yamal, Rodri, Olmo, Fabián are set, and Unai Simón has won Spain shootouts before. Yellow cards were wiped after the QF, so the tie starts clean. Identity unchanged: keep the ball, win the midfield, protect the line behind the line — and beat France the way champions do, however many whistles it takes.
